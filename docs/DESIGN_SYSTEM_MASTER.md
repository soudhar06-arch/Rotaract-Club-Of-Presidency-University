# DESIGN_SYSTEM_MASTER.md

## 1. Purpose

This document is the complete visual and interaction bible for the Rotaract Club of Presidency University website. It defines the design philosophy, brand language, system tokens, component standards, page patterns, and motion principles that should guide every future implementation.

This document is not a feature plan and does not describe implementation code. It is a design specification for the product experience.

---

## 2. Design Manifesto

The Rotaract Club of Presidency University should feel like a premium digital product built for public trust, community impact, and future growth.

### Core Principles

- Every design decision must increase clarity, trust, and warmth.
- Every animation must have purpose and meaning.
- Technology should disappear behind the experience.
- Content should always be more important than decoration.
- Simplicity is sophistication.
- White space is a feature, not empty space.
- Accessibility is mandatory, not optional.
- Every interaction should increase confidence and belonging.
- The experience should feel human, modern, and credible.
- The brand should feel ambitious without becoming arrogant.

### Design Philosophy

The product should feel like a bridge between:

- the energy of youth and the maturity of leadership,
- the warmth of community and the precision of modern design,
- the humanity of service and the polish of premium digital experiences.

The result should feel confident, clear, elegant, and deeply trustworthy.

## 6. Visual Language

### Photography Style

Photography should feel:

- Documentary and editorial
- Authentic and human
- Bright, natural, and slightly cinematic
- Focused on real people, action, service, and community

Photography guidelines:

- Prefer real-event imagery over stock clichés.
- Use warm, natural lighting where possible.
- Favor portraits, candid moments, and service scenes.
- Avoid overly saturated or synthetic-looking imagery.
- Use images with emotional context, not just visual decoration.

### Illustration Style

If illustrations are used, they should be:

- minimal and modern
- geometric but not sterile
- soft and friendly
- used sparingly and purposefully

Illustrations should support clarity, not replace real brand personality.

### Iconography

Icons should be:

- simple and consistent
- slightly rounded and human-friendly
- minimal and precise
- used with a clear functional purpose

Use Lucide-style iconography as the default system.

### Card Language

Cards should feel:

- calm and structured
- premium and editorial
- lightweight but confident
- visually distinct without becoming busy

Card principles:

- One clear idea per card
- Strong hierarchy within the content block
- Ample whitespace around body content
- Avoid too many competing visual elements

### Spacing Language

Spacing should create calm and clarity.

- Generous whitespace around major sections
- Consistent rhythm between text blocks and components
- Enough room for content to breathe
- Avoid cramped layouts or dense interfaces

### Shape Language

The product should use:

- rounded but disciplined forms
- gentle curves rather than harsh corners
- soft corners for cards and surfaces
- subtle asymmetry only where it supports storytelling

### Shadow Language

Shadows should feel:

- light and elegant
- subtle rather than heavy
- used to create depth and hierarchy, not decoration

### Border Radius

Rounded surfaces should feel refined and approachable. Borders should be visible but restrained.

### Depth

Depth should be created through:

- low-contrast layering
- soft shadows
- subtle elevation
- careful separation of sections

Depth should remain understated, never noisy.

### Contrast

Contrast should be used intentionally for:

- hierarchy
- emphasis
- readability
- accessibility

The system should avoid harsh contrast and instead favor balanced, polished contrast.

### Texture

Texture should be minimal.

- Use subtle noise or grain only if it enhances authenticity.
- Prefer clean surfaces over decorative texture.

### Glass Usage

Glass surfaces can be used sparingly for:

- overlays
- navigation polish
- floating panels
- modals and drawers

Glass should remain subtle and refined, not frosted or overdone.

### Gradient Usage

Gradients should be restrained.

- Use only for soft highlights or atmospheric accents.
- Avoid heavy or flashy color transitions.
- Keep gradients aligned with the branded blue and gold palette.

### Color Usage

Color should be used with discipline:

- Blue for trust, clarity, and leadership
- Gold for warmth, recognition, and energy
- Neutral surfaces for calm and legibility
- Semantic colors only where meaning is required

- Success: #10B981
- Warning: #F59E0B

### Usage Rules

- Use Accent Blue as the primary brand anchor in CTAs, active states, and leadership moments.
- Use Rotary Gold as a highlight accent for emphasis and celebration.

All text colors must meet WCAG AA contrast targets. Do not place low-contrast text on light or glass surfaces.

## 12. Motion Design

### Motion Principles

- Motion should feel subtle, purposeful, and premium.
- It should guide, not distract.
- It should reinforce hierarchy and continuity.
- It should feel inspired by Apple and Framer: calm, polished, and deliberate.

### Duration Tokens

- Fast: 150ms
- Standard: 220ms
- Smooth: 300ms
- Slow: 450ms

### Easing

- Standard easing: cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1)
- Enter easing: cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)
- Exit easing: cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1)

### Motion Guidelines by Area

#### Hero

- Fade and rise gently on load
- Use subtle parallax only if it remains elegant and minimal

#### Cards

- Hover lift of 2px to 4px
- Soft shadow increase
- Transition duration: 220ms

#### Buttons

- Small scale 1.01 or 1.03 on hover
- Press feedback with scale 0.97 on active

#### Navigation

- Smooth underline or highlight transitions
- Stable and low-noise motion

#### Forms

- Input focus should feel crisp and calm
- Validation states should animate gently

#### Gallery

- Image reveal with soft fade and slight scale
- Lightbox transitions should feel fluid and unobtrusive

#### Dashboard

- Panel transitions should remain fast and efficient
- Keep motion minimal so focus remains on data and action

#### Images

- Subtle zoom or reveal on enter
- Never overdo motion on media

#### Page Transitions

- Fade + slight content movement
- Avoid full-screen theatrics

### Reduced Motion

Respect prefers-reduced-motion.

- Disable non-essential transitions
- Replace motion with immediate state changes
- Keep interaction feedback visible and accessible

## 13. Microinteractions

### Buttons

- Hover: slight elevation and color deepening
- Focus: visible ring in gold
- Active: brief pressed feedback

### Inputs

- Focus: border and ring shift to blue
- Error: brief shake or color change with accessible message
- Success: subtle success indicator

### Cards

- Hover: elevate slightly and show a precise shadow change
- Click: subtle press state

### Navigation

- Hover: label and icon emphasis
- Active: clear color and underline treatment

### Gallery

- Hover: image zoom or opacity shift
- Lightbox open/close: smooth fade and scale

### Timeline

- Hover: item highlight and soft motion

### Calendar

- Hover states should remain understated and clear

### Dashboard

- Row hover, filter chip states, and action button feedback should be fast and clean

### Dropdowns and Dialogs

- Animate in with soft fade+scale
- Preserve focus management and accessibility

## 14. Responsive Strategy

### Desktop

- Use spacious layouts with generous whitespace
- Two-column and three-column content patterns where appropriate
- Navigation should remain clear and structured

### Laptop

- Maintain the same visual hierarchy with slightly tighter spacing
- Optimize for efficient scanning of content

### Tablet

- Use simplified two-column arrangements where possible
- Cards should remain readable with reduced padding where necessary

### Mobile

- Prioritize single-column flow
- Use large touch targets and simplified navigation patterns
- Avoid cramped content blocks
- Maintain consistent spacing and strong hierarchy

### Responsive Rules

- Preserve the brand expression across all breakpoints.
- Do not allow the design to become overly dense on smaller screens.
- Reduce visual clutter while preserving hierarchy.

---

## 15. Accessibility Rules

Accessibility is a foundational requirement.

### Keyboard

- All interactive elements must be reachable by keyboard.
- Focus states must remain visible and clear.
- Focus order must be logical and predictable.

### Focus

- Focus rings should be visible and high-contrast.
- Do not remove focus outlines.

### Contrast

- Text and control contrast must meet WCAG AA.
- Do not rely on color alone for meaning.

### Reduced Motion

- Respect the user’s motion preferences.
- Provide non-animated alternatives where needed.

### Touch Targets

- Minimum target size: 44px.
- Larger targets for primary actions.

### ARIA

- Use semantic landmarks.
- Ensure dialogs, drawers, and menus expose correct roles and labels.
- Provide accessible names for icon-only controls.

### Semantic HTML

- Use proper heading hierarchy.
- Use lists where appropriate.
- Use landmarks for navigation, main content, and footer.

## 16. Component Design System

The product should use a reusable design system built around clarity, consistency, and warmth.

### Buttons

Button design rules:

- Primary button uses Accent Blue and white text
- Secondary button uses outlined blue treatment
- Ghost button uses transparent surface with subtle hover
- Buttons should have rounded corners and reliable spacing
- Icon buttons must maintain accessible hit areas

Variants:

- Primary
- Secondary
- Ghost
- Destructive
- Icon
- Full-width mobile action

### Cards

Card principles:

- Clean border and subtle shadow
- Consistent padding and spacing
- Strong title and supporting text hierarchy
- Optional image block or badge treatment

Variants:

- Standard card
- Feature card
- Event card
- Profile card
- Statistics card

### Forms

Form principles:

- Inputs should feel calm and trustworthy
- Labels should be crisp and visible
- Validation should be clear and immediate
- Errors should not feel punitive

### Inputs

- Rounded corners with subtle border
- Clear focus state in blue/gold ring
- Large enough touch targets
- Good spacing and clear labels

### Typography Components

Use a consistent component hierarchy for:

- Heading
- Text block
- Lead copy
- Caption
- Label
- Muted text

### Navigation

Navigation should feel premium and clear.

- Navigation should remain simple and calm
- Active state should be obvious but not loud
- Desktop nav should feel editorial; mobile nav should feel purposeful

### Timeline

Use a strong vertical rhythm and moderate spacing.

- Each item should feel distinct but not heavy
- Use subtle color accents for milestones and important points

### Statistics

Statistics should feel credible and polished.

- Use clean numbers with supporting labels
- Avoid visual clutter or excessive ornamentation

### Avatar

Avatars should be circular and simple.

- Consistent size system
- Clean fallback state
- Strong contrast for profile images

### Badge

Badges should feel small and purposeful.

- Use for status, category, or distinction
- Keep them concise and legible

### Modal / Drawer / Tooltip / Accordion / Tabs

These should follow the same visual language as the rest of the system:

- calm surface treatment
- strong spacing rhythm
- accessible focus states
- minimal motion

### Section and Container

Use section spacing and container width consistently across the product.

- Containers should respect maximum content width
- Section spacing should feel deliberate and rhythmic

### Footer and Navbar

The footer and navbar should feel polished and deliberate.

- Navigation should feel calm and trustworthy
- Footer should communicate continuity and community

## 17. Page Design Language

### Home

The home page should feel like a premium editorial experience. It should move from introduction to evidence, then toward invitation.

### About

About should feel grounded, human, and credible. It should emphasize mission, leadership, and service.

### Events

Events should feel lively and organized. The experience should make participation feel easy and exciting.

### Gallery

Gallery should feel warm, emotional, and community-centered. It should present the human side of the club.

### Projects

Projects should feel thoughtful and outcome-driven. Emphasize service, impact, and initiative.

### Awards

Awards should feel respected and distinguished. The design should highlight achievement with elegance rather than spectacle.

### Board

Board should feel confident and professional. The layout should support credibility and trust.

### Join

Join should feel welcoming, clear, and low-friction. The experience must reduce intimidation.

### Collaborate

Collaborate should feel strategic and relationship-based. The page should communicate openness and professionalism.

### Contact

Contact should feel direct, reliable, and easy to use. It should help visitors feel they can reach the club confidently.

### AI Assistant

The AI assistant should feel helpful, calm, and trustworthy. It should appear as a supportive layer, not a disruptive widget.

The admin dashboard should feel fast, minimal, professional, and powerful. It should look like a tool designed for serious work.

Every page should feel like part of the same product. The same visual language, contrast principles, spacing rhythm, and motion rules should apply everywhere.

## 18. Admin Dashboard Design Language

The dashboard should be inspired by Linear, GitHub, Vercel, and Supabase, but remain distinct and aligned with the club’s identity.

### Visual Characteristics

- Fast and minimal
- Calm but efficient
- Structured and high-trust
- Designed for action and clarity
- Professional without feeling corporate or cold

### Layout Principles

- Clear sidebar navigation
- Purposeful page headers
- Strong content hierarchy
- Calm data surfaces and tables
- Clean whitespace and focused layouts

### Dashboard Surface Language

- White or very light surfaces
- Soft borders and low-noise shadows
- Consistent spacing and data density
- Clear status states and action affordances

### Dashboard Motion

- Fast and lightweight
- Subtle transitions only
- No excessive animation in data-heavy interfaces

## 19. Implementation Guardrails

The following are design guardrails for future implementation:

- Do not use generic template aesthetics.
- Do not rely on heavy ornamentation or excessive gradients.
- Do not use stock-looking, overly bright, or synthetic visual language.
- Do not allow decorative motion to overpower content.
- Do not let the interface feel too corporate, too playful, or too academic.
- Every page must feel rooted in the same confidence, warmth, and clarity.

## 20. Final Design Objective

The final product should feel like a modern, premium, trustworthy, and human digital institution for Rotaract. It should make visitors feel that the club is organized, active, impactful, and welcoming. It should strengthen the organization’s identity and create confidence before any feature is even used.
